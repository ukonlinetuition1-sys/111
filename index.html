The teaching cycle

Diagnose. Explain. Practise. Improve.

A lesson should do more than fill an hour. Every stage has a purpose and should make the next stage of learning easier.

Diagnose

Find the misconception, missing knowledge or exam habit that is causing the problem.

Explain

Break the idea down clearly, using examples and questions that expose whether it has really been understood.

Practise

Move from supported examples to independent work, with the level of challenge adjusted in real time.

Improve

Use precise feedback to fix the weakest part, then revisit it until the improvement becomes reliable.

Common questions

What parents usually want to know first.

Clear answers about lesson format, suitability and what happens after an enquiry.

Is tuition 1:1 or in groups?

Both formats are possible. The right choice depends on the pupil, subject and available group fit. Small groups are kept focused so pupils still receive direct feedback.

Can you work from school homework or handwritten work?

Yes. Existing work is often useful diagnostic evidence because it shows how the pupil is currently approaching a task, not just whether an answer is right or wrong.

What happens in the first lesson?

The first lesson is used to establish the pupil’s current level, identify the highest-value priorities and begin teaching immediately rather than spending the whole session on a formal assessment.
