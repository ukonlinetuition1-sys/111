How It Works

A lesson with a clear job to do.

The aim is not to cover content for the sake of it. Each lesson identifies the next useful target, teaches it explicitly and checks whether the improvement holds.

Start with evidence, not assumptions.

A pupil might say they are “bad at English” or “stuck on algebra”, but that is not specific enough to teach well. We use their answers, questions and errors to locate the real difficulty.

  • Previous school or exam work can be used immediately.
  • Questions are chosen to expose misconceptions, not just produce marks.
  • The lesson focus can change if a more important gap appears.

What this avoids

Spending weeks following a generic scheme that revises things the pupil already understands while the actual problem remains untouched.

The four-part cycle

The same structure scales from a Year 6 comprehension lesson to GCSE exam technique.

Diagnose

Pin down the obstacle with targeted questioning and real work.

Explain

Model the thinking clearly and make hidden steps explicit.

Practise

Use carefully sequenced tasks to move from support to independence.

Improve

Give precise feedback, retry the weak point and record the next target.

What a typical lesson feels like.

Structured enough to have momentum, flexible enough to respond to the pupil.

01

Reconnect

Brief retrieval from the previous lesson and a quick check of any homework or school work.

02

Teach

Clear explanation, modelling and questioning around one or two tightly chosen objectives.

03

Apply

Supported then independent practice, with challenge adjusted as understanding becomes clearer.

04

Lock it in

Feedback, correction and a final check that the pupil can now do what they could not do at the start.
